a note from the teacher behind this list

Why I put this together

I started paying closer attention to how much continuous screen time my students were logging in a single day. On some days it worked out to seven hours in a row in front of a screen at school, and probably more once they were home. That number is what got me looking harder at the research, including The Digital Delusion by Horvath, The Anxious Generation by Haidt, and The Opt-Out Family by Loechner, and at what I was doing in my own lessons.

I am now much more careful about when I ask students to use a phone or laptop, and when the more useful move is to put the device away. What surprised me is how readily students respond to that. Given a hands-on, screen-free task, they tend to lean into it rather than resist it. This list is the collection of what has worked for me, shared in case it is useful for your own lessons too.

Why swap at all

the case for putting the screen down

Screens are efficient. They are also easy to overuse for tasks that do not need them: a quick reflection, a group discussion, a first draft. When every task defaults to a device, students lose practice with skills a screen quietly does for them, reading a room, holding a thought long enough to write it down, arguing a point out loud.

None of the activities here are anti-technology. They are simply the version of a task that does not need a screen to work, chosen because paper, movement, and conversation are sometimes the better tool for the job, not the fallback for when the tool is unavailable.
